Call switching with a parked-walls channel

Every call is exactly two walls, each a 140-inch screen. One wall can move from its current partner to a different wall in one action. Both walls it leaves behind are parked, still connected and ready to be reconnected, never dropped.

Scenario: A↔B and C↔DEvery call has exactly 2 participants, both wallsLiveKit video onlyUpdated 8 Oct 2026

What changed from v1

Join mode and group calls are removed, so there is no swap-or-join question. A switch always takes the target wall out of its call. Because a wall has no keyboard, a paired Wall Controller (phone or tablet) now starts the switch.

What gets built

Two new tables (WallSession, IdleSession), three endpoints, one read-only lookup on telepresence-portal, a Parked screen on every wall, and a Wall Controller page. Call and CallParticipant do not change.

Current state

Nothing links a Xyne call to a telepresence wall in either database. A wall is a kiosk Chrome browser that opens a URL, so Xyne sees it as an ordinary user.

How a wall joins today

A kiosk Chrome with a persistent profile logs in and opens /<workspaceId>/chat/<route>/<channelId>?autoJoin=1&mic=…&camera=…&telepresence=1 (PR-029). The telepresence=1 flag only turns on Presentation Mode in the frontend. The backend row it creates is the same as any other user's.

Calls run on LiveKit with one room per Call, created 1:1 by liveKitService.ts. There is no multi-room or room-bridging code.

What exists to build on, and what does not

Missing

No mid-call idle state. Every "idle" in roomMachine.ts and callMachine.ts is a pre-join lifecycle state.

Missing

The "+1 call waiting" banner in IncomingCallModal ends the current call before joining the new one. That forced end is what this project replaces.

Missing

No way for a person to control a wall. The wall is a kiosk browser with no input device, so nobody can press a button on it.

Usable

Wall.wallType already has a SWITCHABLE value next to PRIMARY. It is declared and never read, and it is confirmed to exist for this feature.

UX flow

A person standing at Wall A uses the Wall Controller on a phone or tablet. The wall screen only shows the call and its status. Step through the six moments below. Dark panels are the proposed UI: wall screens on the left, the controller on the right.

Rules the flow follows

  1. Switching is always one confirmation. The confirm text names which walls will be parked.
  2. The picker lists ongoing calls, parked walls and free walls. Each row shows the wall label and who it is with.
  3. Wall A keeps showing its current partner until the new connection is confirmed. If the switch fails, A is still with B.
  4. Parked walls always tell the room what happened and who can bring them back. A wall never just goes black.
  5. Walls that are not marked SWITCHABLE do not appear in the picker and cannot be parked.

Rules for a 140-inch screen

People stand two to four metres from these screens, and the feed is life-size. That changes what the wall UI may do.

Parked walls

Parked is a real connection that is not part of any call. It is the opposite of hanging up. Today the data model only knows "in call" and "left" (CallParticipant.leftAt). Parked is a third state between them.

Reconnecting a parked wall

Reconnecting is the same action as switching, with a parked wall as the target. From the controller at Wall A, tap a parked wall and confirm. A leaves its current partner (who becomes parked) and connects to the parked wall. There is no separate resume flow.

Timeout

A parked wall that nobody reconnects ends for real and returns to its home screen. Start with 10 minutes, configurable. That number is a placeholder until whoever owns LiveKit capacity gives a real one.

Needs a decision: what "instant" means in LiveKit. A LiveKit participant belongs to one room and tracks cannot move between rooms, so a handshake-free reconnect is not possible. The proposed design keeps each parked wall connected to a per-workspace idle room with a token for the call room already minted. Reconnecting connects to the call room first and leaves the idle room second. That should feel instant, usually well under a second. Please confirm that is acceptable, or measure it before Phase 1.

Architecture

Every new piece lives in Xyne's backend or in the two web UIs. The one cross-system call is a read-only question to telepresence-portal about a wall's type.

Who is allowed to switch

A Wall Controller is paired to exactly one wall. Its token acts as that wall's user, so the backend sees "Wall A asks to switch" and not "a phone asks". Pairing is a one-time step per wall.

Which walls must be switchable

The switch moves up to four walls: A, B (parked), C (parked) and D (target). All of them must be SWITCHABLE. If any is PRIMARY, the switch is refused with a clear reason and nothing changes. Free walls only need A and D checked.

Data model

Call and CallParticipant are untouched. WallSession is the new source of truth for which LiveKit room a wall is actually connected to right now, in a call or parked. IdleSession layers the parked business state (who it's parked from, its timers) on top of a WallSession row instead of tracking its own room SID. Wall lives in another database and is read live, never copied. Every Call has exactly two active participants.

The participant-to-idle link is not a foreign key. It is the switch itself: one participant row stops being active and one idle session starts. The parked wall's previous partner is derived from fromCallId. workspaceId is my addition so visibility can be filtered without a join. WallSession.lastHeartbeatAt is what "connected to the service" actually means: the kiosk and the Wall Controller refresh it on a short interval, and a session whose heartbeat goes stale is what the reconciler (Edge cases) treats as dead, not just a row nobody explicitly closed.

Sequences

Switching A to D (D is in a call with C)

  1. The wall lookup (messages 3 and 4) covers all four walls. If D is free there is no C, so only A, B and D are checked.
  2. A is confirmed in room 2 before leaving room 1. A dropped network never leaves A in neither call.
  3. A database transaction cannot span LiveKit. The rows are written in one transaction after LiveKit confirms, and a small reconciler repairs any wall found in the idle room without an IdleSession row, or the reverse.
  4. Call 2 stays alive as A's call with D. Call 1 ends because both its walls have left it.

Reconnecting a parked wall

This is the same sequence with the parked wall as the target. The atomic claim moves from "claim Call 2" to the idle row below, and the wall's old partner (if still in a call) is parked as usual.

State model

Three persisted states: idle, resumed, expired. A reconnected wall is a normal call participant again. An un-reconnected one ends at the timeout and returns to its home screen.

Reconnect and timeout are two writers to the same row. Both use WHERE status='idle', so exactly one wins.

API and events

Endpoint or eventWhat it does
POST /calls/:callId/switch {targetUserId}Moves the caller's wall to the target wall. The target can be in a call, parked or free. No mode field, because there is only one outcome.
GET /idle-sessionsLists parked walls in the caller's workspace, with time parked and the call they came from.
POST /idle-sessions/:id/releaseSends a parked wall to its home screen early.
idle_session.created / .resumed / .expiredPushed on the existing realtime channel to wall kiosks and controllers. No polling.
wall_session.heartbeat (realtime, not REST)Kiosk and Controller push this on a short interval to refresh WallSession.lastHeartbeatAt. A session whose heartbeat goes stale is what the reconciler treats as dead.
GET /api/walls/by-user/:userId (portal)Read-only. Returns {wallType}. Called synchronously for every wall the switch moves.

The atomic claim

-- target is in a call: only one switch may take it
UPDATE calls SET version = version + 1
 WHERE id = $callId AND status = 'active' AND version = $seen;

-- target is parked: only one switch may reconnect it
UPDATE idle_sessions SET status = 'resumed', resumed_into_call_id = $1
 WHERE id = $id AND status = 'idle';

-- 0 rows affected  =>  409 target_unavailable, controller shows "just taken"

The timeout job uses the same predicate with status='expired'.

Why a separate machine

roomMachine and callMachine model one call's lifecycle. A parked wall has no call but still holds a connection. That needs its own owner.

Still open: which transport carries today's join and leave updates. It was not identified. The design requires push, so confirm the channel, likely a workspace-keyed websocket, before Phase 1.

Access control

The Parked list and the picker only show walls in the same workspace as the controller. Every boundary this design touches, Channel and Call, is already workspace-scoped, and an org-wide "which walls are free right now" list would be a new privacy surface.

Needs a yes or no. Scope was confirmed as needed but not chosen. Workspace is used as the safer default until someone confirms.
